Ways to Teach Skeletal System
Pick the ideas that suit your class. Then follow the plan, or use the sheets in any order you like.
Try it in class
Find your own bones. Students gently feel for
bones on themselves, such as the clavicle,
sternum, ribs, mandible and ulna, and find each
one on the labeled diagram.
Build a paper skeleton. Students lay out a
skeleton on dark paper with cotton swabs or
paper strips. Then they label as many bones as
they can from the diagram.
Test two joints. Students bend an elbow, then
circle an arm at the shoulder. Which joint bends
one way, like a door hinge, and which turns
many ways?
Look at X-rays. Show a few X-ray pictures from
a book or the web. Students name the bones
they see, using the labeled diagram.

## Your Skeleton
Read the text. Then answer the questions on the next page.
Without bones, your body would be a soft heap on the
floor. Your skeleton is the frame that holds you up and
gives your body its shape. An adult has 206 bones. They
come in many sizes, from tiny bones inside the ear to the
femur, the long bone in the thigh.
## 1
Bones also protect the soft organs inside you. The skull
is a hard case around the brain. The ribs curve around
the chest and form a cage that guards the heart and
lungs. The spine is a column of small bones called
vertebrae. It holds the body upright, and the spinal cord
runs safely inside it.
## 2
Bones cannot move on their own. Muscles move them.
The place where two bones meet is called a joint. To
bend your arm, a muscle first gets shorter. As it shortens,
it pulls on a bone. The bone then moves at the joint.
Tough bands called ligaments hold the bones of a joint
together. Smooth cartilage covers the ends of the bones so they do not grind against
each other.
## 3
A bone is not solid all the way through. The outside is hard and dense. Inside many bones
is a soft tissue called bone marrow. Red bone marrow makes new blood cells. Bones
also store calcium, a mineral that makes them hard.
## 4
Bones are alive. They have blood vessels, and they grow as you grow. A broken bone can
even repair itself by building new bone across the break. You can help your bones stay
strong. Eat foods with calcium, such as milk and yogurt, and stay active. Bones get
stronger when they are used.

Part B. Answer the questions.
1.A doctor often puts a cast on a broken arm. How does a cast help the bone heal?
It holds the broken ends still and in line, so the new bone joins them in the right place.
2.How does this process show that bones are alive?
Bones have blood vessels and can grow new tissue to repair themselves. Something that is not
alive cannot do that.

The body needs calcium to build the hard new bone that takes the place of the soft tissue.
